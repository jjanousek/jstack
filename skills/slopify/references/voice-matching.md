# Voice matching from a sample

Match observable writing traits without copying the sample's content, claims, personal history, or distinctive phrases.

## Minimum inputs

Use:

- a writing sample;
- the new text's purpose and audience;
- the facts or source material the draft may use;
- any required length, format, or call to action.

A short sample can support a light match, not a confident imitation. If the sample is under roughly 150 words, keep the result conservative and mention the limitation when it matters.

## Build a voice fingerprint

Infer only traits supported by the sample:

- **Directness**: immediate point or gradual setup;
- **Register**: conversational, neutral, technical, formal, playful;
- **Syntax**: typical sentence length, variation, fragments, questions, parentheticals;
- **Paragraph rhythm**: compact blocks, developed paragraphs, lists, transitions;
- **Vocabulary**: plain or specialised, concrete or abstract, contractions, idioms;
- **Punctuation**: commas, colons, dashes, parentheses, semicolons;
- **Point of view**: first person, second person, institutional voice;
- **Evidence habits**: examples, numbers, citations, caveats, counterarguments;
- **Humour and emphasis**: dry, warm, blunt, restrained, absent;
- **Repeated choices**: traits that occur often enough to be intentional.

Ignore topic-specific nouns and one-off accidents. Do not reproduce typos unless the user explicitly needs a faithful transcription.

## Write in the voice

1. Draft from the supplied facts, not from the sample's subject matter.
2. Apply the strongest three to five voice traits.
3. Preserve natural variation; do not force every sentence to display every trait.
4. Avoid phrases distinctive enough to look copied.
5. Compare the draft with the sample for rhythm, directness, and level of detail.
6. Run the slop audit after voice matching so the result does not inherit generic model habits.

## Keep the match honest

- Do not invent experiences, relationships, emotions, preferences, or results for the apparent speaker.
- Do not claim the sample's author wrote or approved the new text.
- Do not add fake vulnerability, slang, typos, or fragments as proof of humanity.
- Do not turn a distinctive voice into a caricature by repeating its most visible feature.
- If the requested content conflicts with the sample's voice, preserve factual clarity and the medium's needs first.

## Output options

Default to the completed draft.

When useful, precede it with a short voice brief:

```text
Voice brief: direct, moderately informal, varied sentence length, concrete examples, little promotional language.
```

If the source sample is inconsistent, state which traits were stable enough to use and which were not.
